# Competition and pricing layer: sourced evidence (Canadian personal Home and Auto)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Status tags: VERIFIED = page fetched and number read; SNIPPET = seen only in a search-result summary, page not fetched; UNVERIFIED = could not confirm; NOT FOUND = searched, no source.
Access note: fsrao.ca, lautorite.qc.ca, some Canadian Underwriter pages returned 403/404/500 to fetch, so several FSRA/AMF claims rest on secondary sources (flagged).

## 0. Headline conclusions

1. No Canadian regulator was found that bans "price optimization" by name. The CIA (June 2024) states it is not prohibited, but it must be disclosed and justified as "just, reasonable and not unfairly discriminatory". Risk is rising (FSRA UDAP rule, May 2026). Treat WTP-based pricing as a regulatory-exposure feature, not a free one.
2. No verified Canadian number exists for "conversion vs price gap to cheapest quote". Defaults must be assumptions calibrated on own quote data.
3. Expense ratio is about 30% (Definity 2025) to 34% (Intact 2025). Commissions are about 14.5% of net underwriting revenue (Definity 2025, all lines).

## 1. Pricing regulation and price optimization (priority)

### 1.1 Status of price optimization in Canada
- Number: no explicit prohibition. Claim: "The current regulatory environment does not prohibit price optimization, but it is expected that actuaries act with integrity and that such practice should be at least disclosed and the impact well documented and understood to assess if the resulting selected rates are just, reasonable and not unfairly discriminatory." Also: "the absence of regulation against price optimization does not entail this practice will commonly be accepted by regulators in all Canadian jurisdictions."
  Source: Canadian Institute of Actuaries, "Price Optimization in Property and Casualty Pricing", doc. 224062, 5 June 2024, p.4. https://www.cia-ica.ca/app/themes/wicket/custom/dl_file.php?p=344956&fid=344957
  Geography: Canada/Ontario. Status: VERIFIED (PDF read in full). Limit: actuarial body paper, not law; not a legal opinion; status may have changed since mid-2024.
- CIA quotes FSRA's Auto Insurance Data and Analytics Strategy Technical Advisory Committee (2022, "Fair Treatment of Consumers in Uses of Big Data Analytics in Auto Insurance"): "price optimization ... is an economic reality not considered under the actuarial standards of practice. This raises the question of what degree of optimization is reasonable" and "is being used in the insurance industry, and in fact is necessary to some extent for insurers to remain solvent. Regulators need to ensure that it doesn't lead to customers being treated unfairly." Source: same CIA paper p.4. Status: VERIFIED as quoted by CIA; FSRA original not fetched. Limit: advisory committee view, not FSRA rule.
- CIA definitions: "rate plan (or rating factor) optimization ... including non-risk based behavioural components ... is probably the one that is used the most in practice." Example given of accepted practice: "limiting dislocation by reducing rates for more price sensitive customers at renewal." CIA SOP 2610.05: SOP covers indicated rates, not selected rates; deviations for dislocation, regulation, competition, strategy "are compliant with the CIA SOP". Source: same, pp.3-5. VERIFIED. Limit: professional standards, not regulatory approval.
- Comparison cited by CIA: US NAIC task force (2015) found "price elasticity of demand", "propensity to shop", "retention adjustment at an individual level" inconsistent with unfair discrimination law; about 20 states restrict or prohibit (MD, OH, CA, FL, VT). UK FCA banned renewal "price walking" effective 1 Jan 2022. Australia ASIC Report 765 (2023) criticised loyalty-promise pricing. Source: same, p.7. VERIFIED (as reported by CIA). Limit: non-Canadian.

### 1.2 Ontario (FSRA)
- Claim: FSRA plans to use its Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices (UDAP) rule "more expansively" to test whether insurers' data use produces fair consumer outcomes. Jordan Solway (FSRA EVP P&C), quoted: "We haven't used it as expansively, and that's going to change." Price optimization/elasticity not mentioned in the article.
  Source: Canadian Underwriter, "Regulator turns eye towards how insurers use data to set rates", 27 May 2026. https://canadianunderwriter.ca/2026/05/27/regulator-turns-eye-towards-how-insurers-use-data-to-set-rates/ VERIFIED. Geography: Ontario (auto focus). Limit: speaker remarks, no rule text.
- Claim: proposed FSRA rating guidance tests: classification and rates must be "just and reasonable", reasonably predictive of risk or fairly distinguish risks, not impair solvency, not be excessive vs applicant's finances. Positive capping encouraged; negative capping not permitted. Credit information prohibited as an auto rating factor under the UDAP rule.
  Source: FSRA Automobile Insurance Rating and Underwriting Supervision Guidance (proposed 2024) as summarised in search results; https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/auto-insurance/regulatory-framework/guidance-auto-insurance/automobile-insurance-rating-and-underwriting-guidance and McCarthy summary https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/publications/fsra-releases-proposed-guidance-automobile-insurance-rating-and-underwriting-supervision-guidance . Status: SNIPPET only (both pages 403/429 on fetch); final status/effective date UNVERIFIED. Limit: "negative capping not permitted" is directly relevant to any engine that discounts renewals for the price-sensitive; confirm in primary text.
- Claim: FSRA standard filing: oversight "will focus on outputs (price) over inputs, costs and methodology"; benchmarks used when insurer data not credible; "file and use" route reported as planned (rates implemented in days within FSRA boundaries). Source: FSRA Standard Filing page https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/auto-insurance/regulatory-framework/guidance-auto-insurance/standard-filing ; Canadian Underwriter "ontario regulator considering file and use for private passenger auto". Status: SNIPPET; current operation of file-and-use UNVERIFIED. Limit: auto only; home rates in Ontario are not rate-approved (general knowledge, UNVERIFIED in this session).
- Take-all-comers: FSRA guidance (15 Nov 2021) says auto insurers must give all Ontario consumers timely quotes "at the lowest possible price". Status: SNIPPET. Limit: auto only.

### 1.3 Quebec (AMF)
- Claim: no AMF guideline or ban on "optimisation des prix" was found in French or English searches. NOT FOUND. Do not state Quebec is permissive; absence of search hits is not evidence.
- Claim: all insurers must file their auto rate manual with the AMF; AMF publishes an annual auto pricing report (2025 edition exists; private chartered insurers wrote 61% of C$7.3B auto premiums in Quebec). Source: AMF, "Rapport annuel Tarification assurance automobile 2025" https://lautorite.qc.ca/fileadmin/lautorite/grand_public/publications/organisation/rapports-annuels/autorite/Rapport-annuel-Tarification-assurance-automobile-2025.pdf ; https://lautorite.qc.ca/en/professionals/insurers/automobile-insurance/ . Status: SNIPPET (403 on fetch). Limit: filing is not the same as approval; approval regime UNVERIFIED.
- AMF has a fair-commercial-practices framework (2013 guideline) and 2025-26 consultations on AI oversight (BLG, July 2025: https://www.blg.com/fr/insights/2025/07/quebecs-autorite-des-marches-financiers-moves-on-ai-oversight-for-financial-institutions ). Status: SNIPPET. Limit: AI governance, not pricing ban.
- Quebec structural fact (background knowledge, UNVERIFIED this session): bodily injury auto cover is public (SAAQ), so private Quebec auto premiums are lower and not comparable to Ontario/Alberta.

### 1.4 "Best terms pricing" (adjacent, real prohibitions)
- Claim: BC (end 2020) enacted "a prohibition against insurers implementing best terms pricing" for strata subscription policies; Alberta adopted a comparable one; CCIR (Dec 2020) signalled review of best terms pricing across P&C. Source: Cassels, "Inside Insurance: Best Terms Pricing..." https://cassels.com/insights/inside-insurance-best-terms-pricing-lazy-underwriting-regulatory-crackdown-and-how-in-house-counsel-can-help/ VERIFIED. Limit: commercial strata, not personal lines; shows regulators will act on price-practice theories.

Engine implication: (a) keep the actuarial indicated price separate from any elasticity-based adjustment; (b) log the deviation and its rationale (CIA expects disclosure); (c) cap deviations; (d) make the WTP layer switchable per province/line; (e) avoid individual-level retention loading for Ontario auto and for renewals vs new business until FSRA final guidance is read.

- Lifetime value: no sourced Canadian figure. Compute internally: LTV = annual margin x expected tenure; an unnamed poll (Canadian Underwriter, 24 Nov 2021, SNIPPET) reports more than half of polled Canadians keep their auto insurer five years or longer, https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/insurance/more-than-half-of-polled-canadians-keep-their-auto-insurer-for-five-years-or-longer-1004214779/ . Implied annual retention of roughly 85% or higher is my inference, UNVERIFIED.
