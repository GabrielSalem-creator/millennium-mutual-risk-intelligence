# Experimentation and creative strategy generation

## How to turn behavioral signals into decisions that can be trusted

### Takeaway
Present every idea as a testable hypothesis, not a discovered truth: show the observed signal, plausible alternative explanations, a before/after treatment, the primary business outcome, and user-protection guardrails. Randomization supports causal claims; dashboards and click/hover associations alone do not.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft’s experimentation guidance recommends defining an objective metric, guardrails, diagnostic metrics, data-quality checks, and a pre-experiment power/sample-size analysis; identity stability and interference between users can affect assignment and analysis. [Microsoft Research: pre-experiment stage](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-pre-experiment-stage/)
- During a test, Microsoft recommends validating data quality and sample-ratio balance, distinguishing objective metrics from diagnostics and guardrails, and accounting for repeated interim looks. [Microsoft Research: during-experiment stage](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-during-experiment-stage/)
- A product metric moving after launch is not by itself evidence the launch caused the change; Microsoft’s North Star discussion emphasizes randomized treatment/control assignment and sufficient sample to make causal attribution. [Microsoft Research: experimentation and the North Star Metric](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/experimentation-and-the-north-star-metric/)
- Repeatedly checking ordinary significance tests and stopping as soon as a result looks positive inflates false-positive risk; use a pre-set horizon or a statistically valid sequential-testing method. [Loecher, “Are Multi-Armed Bandits Susceptible to Peeking?”](https://hrcak.srce.hr/file/295781); [Microsoft Research: during-experiment stage](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-during-experiment-stage/)
- Contextual bandits choose an action for a context and receive reward feedback for the chosen action, so they face partial feedback; randomized action probabilities and logged exposure/reward data matter for evaluating alternatives offline. [Li et al., “A Contextual-Bandit Approach to Personalized News Article Recommendation”](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/p661.pdf); [Vowpal Wabbit estimators](https://github.com/VowpalWabbit/estimators)
- GrowthBook is an open-source experimentation platform supporting experiment assignment through feature flags, SDKs, a visual editor, or custom assignment, and analysis against a connected data source. [GrowthBook open guide to A/B testing](https://docs.growthbook.io/assets/files/open-guide-to-ab-testing.v1.0-228e9312b957a9716766cd8887b18a11.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Recommended signal card:** “Signal → what else could explain it? → testable change → why it may help → primary metric → guardrails → evidence status.” Reserve “we learned” for validated results; label pre-test cards “hypothesis” or “needs instrumentation.”
- Prefer account/user-level assignment that persists across visits; check sample-ratio mismatch, missing event joins, bot/employee traffic, and cross-device identity before interpreting an uplift. If users affect one another or share a workspace, consider assignment at the team/account level.
- Power the test for a minimum practically meaningful change, not just any detectable change. Pre-register the primary metric and window; use secondary metrics to explain mechanisms, not to fish for a win.
- For adaptive optimization, first establish trustworthy candidate variants in randomized tests. Then use a contextual bandit only where feedback arrives quickly and safely; retain a randomized holdout and log each action’s selection probability, context, and outcome. Avoid optimizing a short-term click proxy if it can harm retention, refund rate, satisfaction, or contribution margin.
- Open tooling is implementation infrastructure, not a guarantee of sound experimental design. GrowthBook can manage assignment/analysis; Vowpal Wabbit provides contextual-bandit algorithms; the estimator package can evaluate policies from logged data. Selection of a tool does not validate data quality or causal assumptions.

### Gaps
- No VDO-X event schema, baseline rate, eligible traffic, identity model, or current conversion/retention data was available in this research task. Consequently no VDO-X behavioral observation or expected lift can be claimed.
- Sample-size requirements and test duration must be calculated from VDO-X baseline rates, variance, chosen minimum effect, allocation, and follow-up window; no generic number is defensible.

## Example observation → competing explanations → experiment cards for the deck

### Takeaway
The examples below are deliberately hypotheses, not claims that VDO-X visitors already show these behaviors. Each pairs a visually legible before/after idea with a way to learn whether it helps, and avoids treating attention as purchase intent.

### Cited Findings
- In a randomized field study of a non-advertising app, the effects of a hard paywall, a soft landing with limited free use, and paid-only offerings depended on how these monetization choices were combined; the researchers report large field experiments and an interaction effect. The population and product differ from VDO-X, so it motivates testing combinations rather than assuming a universal paywall rule. [Cao, Chintagunta & Li, “From Free to Paid”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00222437221131562)
- In a two-year randomized experiment involving 2.1 million European newspaper readers, auto-renew offers increased short-run subscriptions but recipients were less likely to become paid subscribers at any point over the longer horizon than those offered auto-cancel subscriptions. This is evidence that short-run conversion can diverge from durable value, not a direct estimate for video tools. [DellaVigna et al., “Sophisticated Consumers with Inertia”](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Sophisticated-Consumers-with-Inertia.pdf)
- The FTC identifies false urgency, hidden fees, and interfaces that trick users into revealing data or make cancellation difficult as dark-pattern concerns. [FTC report on dark patterns](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers)
- NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework includes privacy, autonomy, transparency, fairness, accountability, and managing harmful bias among trustworthy-AI considerations. [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)

### Inferences
These are illustrative cards to adapt after inspecting real VDO-X pages and event data. “Before” and “after” refer to candidate UI renderings; they are not screenshots or performance results.

1. **Plan attention: measure exposure before interpreting hover.** *Hypothetical observation:* pointer dwell or repeated detail opening is higher on plan 3. *Other explanations:* it is larger/more visually prominent, appears first or above the fold, has confusing wording, or cursor movement is incidental. *Before → after:* three equal plan cards in one row → same plans in a readable stacked comparison with equal prominence and a clear “compare features” control. Keep price, order, and copy fixed for the first test. Instrument eligible exposure, viewport visibility, detail opens, and plan CTA. Primary outcome: paid contribution per eligible visitor; guardrails: plan comprehension, refund/cancellation, and completion. On touch devices, report visible-in-viewport time as exposure opportunity, not “hover” or attention.

2. **Mobile plan discovery: make the comparison legible.** *Hypothetical observation:* mobile visitors scroll past plan cards without opening details. *Other explanations:* page is slow, plan names are unclear, the user is only researching, or pricing is not yet relevant. *Before → after:* cramped three-column cards → vertical cards with a persistent, neutral “compare plans” anchor and the same complete terms. Test on mobile only with randomized eligible visitors. Primary: qualified plan selection or paid contribution; guardrails: time to find plan, abandonment, accessibility, and support contacts. Visibility and scroll depth explain exposure, not preference.

3. **Price/credit comprehension: replace guesswork with a calculator.** *Hypothetical observation:* users repeatedly open credit details or leave after seeing a credit balance. *Other explanations:* generation quality, latency, unclear credit expiry, or an unexpected result may be the friction. *Before → after:* plan names and a credit total → a concise calculator showing estimated credits per typical video, remaining balance, and full price/terms before purchase. Test explanatory clarity against existing copy while keeping actual prices identical. Primary: completed purchase and net contribution; guardrails: surprise refunds, support requests, and successful first generation.

4. **Paywall timing: test a clear post-result prompt.** *Hypothetical observation:* exits cluster near free-credit depletion or immediately after first successful generation. This is not proof that a “craving moment” exists. *Other explanations:* credits run out before value is demonstrated, output quality varies, or generation fails/feels slow. *Before → after:* surprise interruption during creation → after the promised free result, show a transparent balance and optional upgrade prompt with price and what credits buy. Compare against an unobtrusive balance reminder, using the same offers. Primary: net contribution per randomized new user over a defined period; guardrails: first-result success, repeat use, refunds, and complaint rate. Never obscure price or make the user lose work to force a purchase.

5. **Choose by stated job, not inferred wealth or age.** *Hypothetical observation:* users visit both “single video” and “batch campaign” feature descriptions before choosing a plan. *Other explanations:* plan names are unclear or feature comparisons are incomplete. *Before → after:* generic tier cards → optional “What are you making?” choices (one-off clip, regular publishing, batch work) followed by an explanation of which plan features fit that self-selected job; all plans remain visible. Test whether this improves successful plan choice and durable use. Guardrails: opt-out, equal access to essential terms, wrong-fit refunds, and subgroup outcome checks where lawful and privacy-preserving. Do not infer sensitive traits or purchasing power from browsing behavior.

6. **When to ask for preferences: ask at a useful moment.** *Hypothetical observation:* visitors dismiss an early country/age pop-up. *Other explanations:* it interrupts exploration, the question feels invasive, or the value of answering is unclear. *Before → after:* mandatory interrupting modal → optional, dismissible question only when it materially changes a feature (for example, locale/currency) with a one-line explanation and a non-personalized path. Test response rate and task completion, not data capture alone. Minimize collection; avoid age unless genuinely needed for a defined legal/product requirement. Guardrails: completion, opt-out, privacy complaints, and deletion requests.

7. **Offer design: make savings verifiable.** *Hypothetical observation:* visitors inspect discounts but do not proceed. *Other explanations:* total price, renewal terms, or plan fit is unclear; a discount badge could be distracting. *Before → after:* a vague “save” badge → transparent total, billing interval, renewal/cancellation terms, and the arithmetic behind any discount. If testing an actual offer, randomize the price/offer separately from layout and measure margin and longer-term paid use, not conversion alone. Guardrails: refunds, chargebacks, cancellations, and parity/fairness review. Never invent scarcity or a reference price.

8. **Adaptive presentation: personalize explanation, not access.** *Hypothetical observation:* different self-selected tasks lead to different feature-detail engagement. *Other explanations:* device, traffic source, or content clarity drives the split. *Before → after:* one generic feature explanation → the same product and prices explained around the user’s selected job or current session context. Start with randomized, transparent rules; only consider a bandit after candidate messages and outcomes are validated. Keep a stable control and audit assignment probabilities. Guardrails: plan/price equality, opt-out, comprehension, retention, and satisfaction.

- **Do not implement demographic extraction or exploitative targeting:** age, wealth, or “buyer likelihood” inferred from clicks should not be used to reduce free credits, personalize price, or withhold value. NIST highlights privacy, autonomy, and harmful-bias risks; FTC material identifies manipulative interface practices as consumer-protection concerns. Use voluntary task preferences and current product context, give everyone clear prices and terms, and make personalization optional. [NIST AI RMF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf); [FTC dark-pattern report](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers)

### Gaps
- These sources do not establish that stacked pricing cards, hover tracking, prompts after a successful video, or job-based copy improve VDO-X revenue. They are designed experiments, not proven winning treatments.
- Exact UI screenshots, accessible event instrumentation, and current terms are needed to make faithful before/after visuals. The app should mark each item as “observed,” “hypothesis,” “testing,” or “validated” and show event counts and windows for genuine observations.
- The cited paywall studies cover different products, markets, and offers; their findings should not be presented as a VDO-X forecast.

## Fair pricing, paywalls, adaptive policies, and measurement

### Takeaway
Optimize durable customer value under transparent terms, not short-term conversion alone. Start with controlled experiments and explicit measurement; adapt cautiously only after enough causal evidence and trustworthy logging.

### Cited Findings
- The randomized “From Free to Paid” study reports that soft landing and paid-exclusive content can each reduce incumbent users’ subscription willingness while their combination has a positive interaction, demonstrating that monetization levers can interact and should be tested jointly or factorially where feasible. [Cao, Chintagunta & Li, “From Free to Paid”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00222437221131562)
- The randomized newspaper paywall study followed users for two years and found short-term subscription and longer-term subscription outcomes moved differently under auto-renew versus auto-cancel offers. [DellaVigna et al., “Sophisticated Consumers with Inertia”](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Sophisticated-Consumers-with-Inertia.pdf)
- Microsoft’s LinUCB paper describes contextual recommendation as selecting an item based on context while learning from observed click feedback, balancing exploration and exploitation. [Li et al., WWW 2010](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/p661.pdf)
- Vowpal Wabbit’s estimator library explains logged contextual-bandit data and off-policy evaluation: evaluation estimates reward for alternative policies from a logging policy’s data, with confidence intervals. [Vowpal Wabbit estimators](https://github.com/VowpalWabbit/estimators)
- FTC’s dark-pattern findings include false urgency, hidden fees, and obstructive cancellation; NIST’s framework calls for privacy-enhanced, transparent, accountable, fair, and reliable systems. [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers); [NIST AI RMF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Treat hover, dwell, scrolling, and visible-in-viewport time as **opportunity/attention proxies**, each affected by layout and device. They do not reveal preference, age, income, or intent by themselves. Instrument the actual event (pointer enter/leave, touch viewport exposure, click, successful generation) and state the denominator.
- For mobile, “which card was visible during a meaningful interval?” is a more defensible exposure signal than claiming hover. Even viewport exposure is not proof the person read or liked it. The experiment can test whether changing card arrangement improves downstream choices.
- To learn whether paywall timing matters, define eligible cohort and treatment trigger ahead of time (for example, successful first generation or credit balance reaching zero), randomize a stable unit, and keep price/terms equivalent. Measure paid contribution over a horizon long enough to capture refunds, repeat use, cancellation, and retention. Show successful free value before a prompt if that is the stated product promise.
- Avoid changing price, order, layout, copy, and timing simultaneously: if the result changes, the team would not know why. Use factorial tests only when traffic/power and interaction questions warrant them.
- Do not segment by age, inferred wealth, ethnicity, or “likely buyer” deductions. Offer voluntary, task-based personalization; keep the product’s essential features and price rules clear and consistent. A user may opt out without losing access to ordinary choices.
- A contextual bandit is a later-stage optimization mechanism, not the discovery engine for unknown causal effects. Start with balanced randomized tests. If moving to bandits, constrain the action set to approved, truthful variants, keep a randomized holdout, log context/action/reward/selection probability, include delayed outcomes and user-value guardrails, and have a rollback threshold.
- A concrete experiment card should distinguish: (a) observation supported by event logs, (b) competing causes, (c) UI intervention, (d) goal and primary metric, (e) guardrails, (f) minimum effect/sample plan, (g) status and confidence. Do not call generated mockups “before/after results.”

### Gaps
- No general evidence found here establishes the optimal moment to sell video-generation credits, a universal preferred pricing layout, or which categories of VDO-X users respond to which offer. Those questions require VDO-X-specific controlled tests.
- Contextual bandits can optimize the logged reward, but the right reward definition (especially long-term satisfaction, refunds, customer lifetime value, and equity constraints) remains a product decision and needs VDO-X data and policy review.
- This research was limited to experiment design, monetization examples, trustworthy personalization, and open-source experimentation tooling; it did not inspect VDO-X or private database contents.
