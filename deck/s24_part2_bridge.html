From analysis to action · Part 2

The same math, run on every new customer before we quote.

In kid words: a new person asks for a price. The machine looks up which group they belong to, works out the average bill for that group, and only says yes if we can charge more than that and still be cheaper than other insurers.
1 APPLICANTProduct, city, age, risk band, deductible, sales source.
→ profile
2 CATEGORYProfile is mapped to its group using the factors learned from the data (e.g. the Montreal water factor).
→ risk factors
3 RANDOM VARIABLEClaims N ~ Poisson, cost X per claim, S = ΣX.
→ model of one year
4 EXPECTATIONE[S] = E[N]·E[X] plus a 14% claims-handling load and a 90% range.
→ expected loss
5 DECIDEBlocked source (B07)? Price needed far above market? Then refer or decline.
→ accept / decline
6 PRICEPick the price that maximises chance-to-win × profit.
→ optimal competitive quote

…then a 1,000-policy simulation shows the book loses money.

Three outcomes

  • ACCEPTwe can match or beat the market and keep our margin.
  • ACCEPT · PREMIUMwe need a price a little above market; the win rate will be lower.
  • DECLINE / REFERcompetitive price loses money in the long run, or the sales source is blocked.

Open platform.html (the live app) to try your own applicant. Expense load, margin and price sensitivity are sliders because the data has no quote or conversion records.

Break-even = expected loss with handling ÷ (1 − 0.30 expense load) = 2,795 × 1.14 ÷ 0.70 = 3,186 ÷ 0.70 ≈ 4,551 (same assumptions as the previous slide; the app recalculates live). The 30% expense load and 14% claims-handling load are sourced assumptions (Definity 2025 expense ratio 29.6%; MSA Research claims-handling cost 8–11 points of premium).
