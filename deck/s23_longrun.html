The long-run math

One customer is luck. A thousand customers is arithmetic.

1 · The random variable

S = X₁ + X₂ + … + XN
N = how many claims this year (a random count). X = what each one costs. S = our total bill for one customer.

Kid words: roll a dice to see how many accidents, then roll again for each price tag.

2 · The expectation

E[S] = E[N] × E[X]
Average number of claims × average cost of a claim. That is the fair "cost of one customer".

Kid words: if you get 1 candy a day, you expect 7 a week, even if some days are zero.

3 · Large numbers

The average of n customers wobbles by about σ/√n. Chebyshev: P(|avg − μ| ≥ ε) ≤ σ²/(n·ε²). More customers → smaller wobble.

Kid words: one coin flip is random; a thousand flips are almost exactly half heads.

Montreal Home · Medium risk · current price ≈ 1,286

THE HOUSE LOSESExpected loss 2,795, or 3,186 with claims-handling cost. Of the 1,286 price, 1,286 × 0.70 = 900 is left after expenses: −2,286 per policy. Break-even price: 3,186 ÷ 0.70 = 4,551. The band narrows around a negative number: by about 33 policies it no longer touches zero, so more customers makes the loss certain.

Calgary Auto · Low risk · current price ≈ 1,234

THE HOUSE WINSExpected loss 424, or 483 with claims-handling cost; 1,234 × 0.70 = 864 is left: +381 per policy. A loss is still possible with a few policies; by about 107 the band stays above zero. Chebyshev (95%, margin = the whole profit) guarantees it at ≈ 553 policies.

How these were computed (node script, applicant age 45–54, $1,000 deductible, Agent channel, not bundled, B07 excluded, from the refit model): Montreal Home claim rates are water 0.325 + other 0.103 per year, Calgary Auto 0.151; expected loss per policy = rate × mean cost per claim = 2,795 and 424. Profit per policy = price × (1 − 0.30) − expected loss × 1.14. I applied the 1.14 claims-handling load to the spread as well (loss with handling = 1.14 × S): σ per policy-year ≈ 5,844 × 1.14 ≈ 6,662 (Montreal Home) and ≈ 1,756 × 1.14 ≈ 2,002 (Calgary Auto). Shaded band = profit ± 1.96 σ/√n; "band stays off zero" n = (1.96σ/profit)²; Chebyshev n = σ²/(0.05 × profit²). The 30% expense load and the 14% claims-handling load are sourced assumptions, not measured from the data: Definity 2025 expense ratio 29.6%; MSA Research claims-handling cost 8–11 points of premium; the industry combined ratio was about 92–95% in 2024 (context from analysis/PATTERNS.md).
