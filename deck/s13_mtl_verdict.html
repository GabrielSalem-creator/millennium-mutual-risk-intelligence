  • Water claims alone explain it: without them Montreal Home runs at 0.37, a profitable line.
  • Excess water cost versus the other cities' water frequency ≈ 0.375M over the period.
  • Toronto charges more (1,416) yet loses less (Montreal Home loses about 1.5× as much per premium dollar).

Expenses are not in the data; any expense load only makes the gap bigger.

Law of large numbers

Annual loss S = X₁ + … + XN, so E[S] = E[N]·E[X]. The average over n policies wobbles by σ/√n, which shrinks as n grows, but it shrinks around the expected value.

If the expected value is below zero, more customers don't rescue us. They make the loss more certain.

Explained simply: flip a coin that loses us 643 each time on average. One flip might win. A thousand flips will not.
