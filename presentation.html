The numbers in plain words

  • Expected loss per Montreal Home policy-year ≈ 1,878; price charged ≈ 1,235. Gap ≈ 643 per policy-year, before any expenses.
  • Water claims alone explain it: without them Montreal Home runs at 0.37, a profitable line.
  • Excess water cost versus the other cities' water frequency ≈ 0.375M over the period.
  • Toronto charges more (1,416) yet loses less (Montreal Home loses about 1.5× as much per premium dollar).

Expenses are not in the data; any expense load only makes the gap bigger.

Law of large numbers

Annual loss S = X₁ + … + XN, so E[S] = E[N]·E[X]. The average over n policies wobbles by σ/√n, which shrinks as n grows, but it shrinks around the expected value.

If the expected value is below zero, more customers don't rescue us. They make the loss more certain.

Explained simply: flip a coin that loses us 643 each time on average. One flip might win. A thousand flips will not.

4 · Cap new business

The long-run math

One customer is luck. A thousand customers is arithmetic.

1 · The random variable

S = X₁ + X₂ + … + XN
N = how many claims this year (a random count). X = what each one costs. S = our total bill for one customer.

Kid words: roll a dice to see how many accidents, then roll again for each price tag.

2 · The expectation

E[S] = E[N] × E[X]
Average number of claims × average cost of a claim. That is the fair "cost of one customer".

Kid words: if you get 1 candy a day, you expect 7 a week, even if some days are zero.

3 · Large numbers

The average of n customers wobbles by about σ/√n. Chebyshev: P(|avg − μ| ≥ ε) ≤ σ²/(n·ε²). More customers → smaller wobble.

Kid words: one coin flip is random; a thousand flips are almost exactly half heads.

Montreal Home · Medium risk · current price ≈ 1,286

THE HOUSE LOSESExpected loss 2,795, or 3,186 with claims-handling cost. Of the 1,286 price, 1,286 × 0.70 = 900 is left after expenses: −2,286 per policy. Break-even price: 3,186 ÷ 0.70 = 4,551. The band narrows around a negative number: by about 33 policies it no longer touches zero, so more customers makes the loss certain.

Calgary Auto · Low risk · current price ≈ 1,234

THE HOUSE WINSExpected loss 424, or 483 with claims-handling cost; 1,234 × 0.70 = 864 is left: +381 per policy. A loss is still possible with a few policies; by about 107 the band stays above zero. Chebyshev (95%, margin = the whole profit) guarantees it at ≈ 553 policies.

How these were computed (node script, applicant age 45–54, $1,000 deductible, Agent channel, not bundled, B07 excluded, from the refit model): Montreal Home claim rates are water 0.325 + other 0.103 per year, Calgary Auto 0.151; expected loss per policy = rate × mean cost per claim = 2,795 and 424. Profit per policy = price × (1 − 0.30) − expected loss × 1.14. I applied the 1.14 claims-handling load to the spread as well (loss with handling = 1.14 × S): σ per policy-year ≈ 5,844 × 1.14 ≈ 6,662 (Montreal Home) and ≈ 1,756 × 1.14 ≈ 2,002 (Calgary Auto). Shaded band = profit ± 1.96 σ/√n; "band stays off zero" n = (1.96σ/profit)²; Chebyshev n = σ²/(0.05 × profit²). The 30% expense load and the 14% claims-handling load are sourced assumptions, not measured from the data: Definity 2025 expense ratio 29.6%; MSA Research claims-handling cost 8–11 points of premium; the industry combined ratio was about 92–95% in 2024 (context from analysis/PATTERNS.md). From analysis to action · Part 2

The same math, run on every new customer before we quote.

In kid words: a new person asks for a price. The machine looks up which group they belong to, works out the average bill for that group, and only says yes if we can charge more than that and still be cheaper than other insurers.
1 APPLICANTProduct, city, age, risk band, deductible, sales source.
→ profile
2 CATEGORYProfile is mapped to its group using the factors learned from the data (e.g. the Montreal water factor).
→ risk factors
3 RANDOM VARIABLEClaims N ~ Poisson, cost X per claim, S = ΣX.
→ model of one year
4 EXPECTATIONE[S] = E[N]·E[X] plus a 14% claims-handling load and a 90% range.
→ expected loss
5 DECIDEBlocked source (B07)? Price needed far above market? Then refer or decline.
→ accept / decline
6 PRICEPick the price that maximises chance-to-win × profit.
→ optimal competitive quote
